Where a Dallas title office loses its day.

Your closing software runs production. The work around it lives in email, on the whiteboard, and in one person's head.

01

"Where's my file?"

Agents and lenders call and email all day asking where a file is. Your escrow officer answers, and the file she was working on stops.

What we'd build

A status link for every file. Agents and lenders open it to see the current stage and what's still outstanding, without calling anyone.

02

Chasing outside parties

Payoffs, surveys, HOA resale certificates, insurance binders, and the CD get chased by email and tracked on a whiteboard.

What we'd build

A request list on each file showing what was asked for, from whom, and when, with follow-up reminders for anything still outstanding.

03

Files stalling in curative

Files sit in curative with nobody watching how long they've been waiting.

What we'd build

A file board that shows how many days each file has sat in its current stage and flags the ones waiting too long.

04

One person holds the process

One senior escrow officer carries the whole process in her head, so the office slows down when she's out.

What we'd build

Her steps written into the file board, so anyone can open a file, see what comes next, and keep it moving.

05

Post-closing follow-up slips

Once a file closes, recorded documents and final policies get forgotten.

What we'd build

Post-closing tasks that stay open on the board until the recorded documents and final policy are back.

Questions Dallas title offices ask.

Do we have to replace RamQuest, SoftPro, Qualia, or ResWare?

No. Your closing software keeps running production. This handles the status questions, outside-party requests, and follow-up that currently live in email and on the whiteboard.

Will it connect to our closing software?

It depends on which system you run and what it allows. Finding that out is one of the first things the Blueprint answers, before a price is set.

You're in Chattanooga. How does that work with a Dallas office?

Over video, on your schedule. Chattanooga is on Eastern time, one hour ahead of Dallas, so the working days overlap almost completely. The Blueprint is a series of screen-shares where we walk through your file process together; nobody needs to fly.

Do you know how Texas title work runs?

We know workflow and integration; you know Texas closings. The Blueprint starts with your process, in your words, and the board gets written around it. Nothing here replaces your escrow officers' judgment, and we won't pretend to be a Texas title agent.

Does it handle wires, trust accounting, or settlement statements?

No. It doesn't touch money.

Do agents and lenders need an account?

No. Each file gets a secure status link they can open without logging in.

Who owns it?

You do. Once the build is paid in full, the system, its source code, and your data belong to you, running on your own domain.

Emberforge brings a set of reusable tools and components to every project, and your system includes a permanent license to use them. If you need exclusivity in your market, that's scoped and priced in the Blueprint.

How long does it take?

The Blueprint usually takes 1–2 weeks. A file board for your team typically takes 4–6 weeks to build. With status links and outside-party reminders, plan on 6–10 weeks.
